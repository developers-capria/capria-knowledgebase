Each portfolio company has a dedicated profile in the knowledge base, serving as the canonical record for all investment-related information about that company. When you need to look up a company's founding year, current stage, lead investors, or exit status, the company profile is your starting point. Keep these profiles accurate and up to date so that every member of the Capria team is working from the same information.

## Company Profile Fields

Maintain the following fields for every portfolio company record. Consistent, complete data across all profiles enables reliable filtering, reporting, and portfolio analysis.

| Field | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Company Name | Legal entity name | Include DBA if different from legal name |
| Founded | Year the company was founded | — |
| Sector / Vertical | Primary industry category | Use the standard Capria sector taxonomy |
| Stage at First Investment | Stage at the time of Capria's initial investment | Record as it was at time of initial investment — do not update retroactively |
| Current Stage | Stage reflecting the company's most recent round | Update after each new funding round |
| Headquarters | City and country of primary operations | — |
| Founders | Key founders and their titles | LinkedIn profiles are helpful for reference |
| Website | Official company URL | — |
| Status | Active, Partial Exit, Full Exit, or Written Off | Update immediately upon any liquidity event or write-off decision |

## Investment Status

Every portfolio company is assigned one of four status categories. Use the tabs below to understand what each status means and what data to prioritize tracking for each.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Active">
    The company is operational and Capria holds a current equity position. This is the default status for all portfolio companies from the time of initial investment until a liquidity event or write-off occurs.

    **Key data to track:**
    - Current valuation (last round or marked-to-market)
    - Fully diluted ownership percentage
    - Most recent operational metrics (ARR, burn rate, runway)
    - Next anticipated financing event or milestone
    - Board seat holder and meeting cadence
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Partial Exit">
    Capria has sold a portion of its stake but retains an equity position in the company. Partial exits occur through secondary sales, tender offers, or partial buyouts.

    **Key data to track:**
    - Proceeds received from the partial sale
    - Remaining ownership percentage (post-sale, fully diluted)
    - Date and terms of the partial exit transaction
    - Remaining carrying value of the unrealized position
    - DPI contribution from the partial exit
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Full Exit">
    Capria has fully exited its position. Full exits occur via IPO, merger or acquisition, or a full secondary sale of Capria's shares.

    **Key data to track:**
    - Exit date and transaction type (IPO, M&A, secondary)
    - Total proceeds received
    - MOIC and IRR on the full investment
    - Any escrow or earnout amounts still pending
    - Fund(s) that held the position and their realized returns
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Written Off">
    The company has failed, ceased operations, or the investment has been written down to zero. This status reflects a loss of value and should be recorded promptly when the decision is made.

    **Key data to track:**
    - Date of write-off decision
    - Final carrying value before write-off
    - Total capital deployed (for loss reporting)
    - Brief narrative on the reason for write-off
    - Any residual IP, assets, or recovery scenarios to monitor
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Sector Taxonomy

Use the following standard sector categories when classifying portfolio companies. Consistent taxonomy ensures accurate portfolio filtering, LP reporting, and sector-level performance analysis across funds.

- **Fintech & Financial Services**
- **Enterprise Software & SaaS**
- **Consumer & Commerce**
- **Health & Life Sciences**
- **Climate & Sustainability**
- **Infrastructure & Deep Tech**
- **Emerging Markets Enablers**

<Tip>
  Always use the standard sector taxonomy rather than creating custom categories. Consistent classification makes it possible to filter by sector across funds, benchmark sector-level performance, and produce accurate LP reports without manual reconciliation.
</Tip>

## Maintaining Company Records

Keep company profiles current by following these steps after each significant event. Stale records create gaps in reporting and can lead to decisions being made on outdated information.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Update after each new funding round">
    Record the new round type, round size, lead investor, post-money valuation, and Capria's updated ownership percentage. Change the company's current stage to reflect the new round.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record leadership and personnel changes">
    Log any changes to the founding team or key executive roles (CEO, CFO, CTO). Note the date of the change and, where available, the incoming leader's name and background.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update valuation after each mark-to-market event">
    Revise the company's carrying value whenever a new round, comparable transaction, or revenue milestone provides a meaningful new data point. Note the methodology used for the updated mark.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log significant milestones">
    Record major events such as product launches, key customer wins, revenue milestones, regulatory approvals, or strategic partnerships. These entries build the operational history of the company and support future due diligence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change status upon exit or write-off">
    Update the company status to Partial Exit, Full Exit, or Written Off as soon as the event is confirmed. Record all relevant financial details at the time of the status change.
  </Step>
</Steps>
