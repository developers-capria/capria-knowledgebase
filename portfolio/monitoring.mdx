Active portfolio monitoring helps the Capria team stay informed about company progress, identify companies that need support, and maintain up-to-date valuations for fund reporting. Monitoring is not a passive exercise — it requires consistent data collection, structured review cycles, and a willingness to engage early when warning signs appear. The cadences, metrics, and processes in this section define how the investment team tracks the health of every active portfolio company across all Capria funds.

## Monitoring Cadence

Use the table below to understand who is responsible for each monitoring activity and how often it occurs. Stick to these cadences to ensure that the knowledge base stays current and that no company goes unreviewed for an extended period.

| Frequency | Activity | Responsible |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Collect MRR/ARR and key operational metrics from portfolio companies | Portfolio team |
| Quarterly | Full portfolio review; update valuations; flag concerns for team discussion | Investment team + CFO |
| Annually | Full company valuation update; update fund NAV for LP reporting | CFO + Auditors |
| Event-driven | Record new funding round, M&A activity, or executive leadership change | Deal team |

## Key Operational Metrics

The right metrics vary depending on the company's business model and stage. Use the tabs below to identify which KPIs to collect and monitor for each type of portfolio company.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="SaaS & Software">
    Software and SaaS companies should be tracked against revenue growth, retention, and unit economics. These metrics reveal whether the business is growing efficiently and retaining the customers it acquires.

    | Metric | Description |
    |---|---|
    | ARR | Annual Recurring Revenue — the annualized value of all active subscription contracts |
    | MRR | Monthly Recurring Revenue — the monthly equivalent, useful for tracking short-term momentum |
    | MoM Growth | Month-over-month MRR or ARR growth rate |
    | Churn Rate | Percentage of revenue or customers lost in a given period |
    | CAC | Customer Acquisition Cost — total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers acquired |
    | LTV | Lifetime Value — the projected total revenue from a customer over their relationship with the company |
    | NRR | Net Revenue Retention — measures expansion, contraction, and churn as a percentage of prior-period revenue |
    | Headcount | Total employee count and growth rate; flag sudden spikes or drops |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Marketplace & Consumer">
    Marketplace and consumer businesses are measured by volume, engagement, and the efficiency with which they acquire and retain users. Focus on whether the platform is growing and whether users are returning.

    | Metric | Description |
    |---|---|
    | GMV | Gross Merchandise Value — total transaction volume flowing through the platform |
    | Take Rate | The percentage of GMV that the company retains as revenue |
    | MAU / DAU | Monthly and Daily Active Users — measures engagement and stickiness |
    | Customer Acquisition Cost | Total marketing and sales spend divided by new users or buyers acquired |
    | Cohort Retention | Percentage of users from a given acquisition period who remain active over subsequent months |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Deep Tech & Hardware">
    Deep tech and hardware companies often operate on longer timelines with milestone-based progress. Track technical and regulatory progress alongside financial health metrics.

    | Metric | Description |
    |---|---|
    | Technical Milestones | Progress against defined R&D or product development milestones (e.g., prototype complete, pilot deployed) |
    | Regulatory Milestones | Status of any required regulatory approvals, certifications, or filings |
    | Burn Rate | Monthly cash outflow; essential for understanding how quickly capital is being consumed |
    | Runway | Months of operation remaining at the current burn rate before the company needs new capital |
    | Key Partnerships | Status of strategic or commercial partnerships that validate the technology or open distribution |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Fintech">
    Fintech companies require monitoring of both commercial performance and regulatory standing. Volume and revenue trends must be evaluated alongside compliance and risk metrics.

    | Metric | Description |
    |---|---|
    | TPV | Total Payment Volume — the total value of transactions processed through the platform |
    | Revenue | Gross and net revenue, broken down by product line where applicable |
    | Regulatory Status | Current licensing and compliance status across all operating jurisdictions |
    | Risk Metrics | Key indicators such as default rate, fraud rate, or loss ratio depending on the product type |
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Valuation Updates

Portfolio company valuations are updated periodically to reflect the current fair market value of Capria's holdings. Use one of the three approaches below, selecting the method best supported by available data. Document the methodology used for each mark alongside the updated valuation figure.

1. **Last Round Valuation** — use the post-money valuation from the company's most recent priced funding round as the carrying value. This is the most straightforward approach and is appropriate when a round has closed recently and no material adverse events have occurred since.

2. **Comparable Transactions** — benchmark the company against recent financing transactions or M&A exits involving similar-stage, similar-sector companies. Apply a discount or premium based on the company's relative growth rate, margin profile, and market position.

3. **Revenue Multiple** — apply a market-derived revenue multiple (e.g., ARR multiple for SaaS) to the company's current revenue or ARR. Reference current public market multiples and recent private transaction data to calibrate the multiple used.

<Note>
  Valuations should be reviewed at least quarterly and updated whenever a significant new data point is available — including a new funding round, a material revenue milestone, a key customer loss, or any adverse event that could affect the company's fair market value. Document all valuation changes in the portfolio company profile.
</Note>

## Monitoring Red Flags

The following conditions require prompt attention. When you identify any of these signals, escalate to the investment team and schedule direct outreach to company management.

<Accordion title="Declining Revenue or Growth Stall">
  Two or more consecutive quarters of flat or declining revenue in a company that is still in growth stage is a significant warning sign. Request an updated set of financials and a management explanation. Assess whether the cause is temporary (market conditions, one-time churn) or structural (product-market fit issues, competitive displacement). Update the carrying value if warranted.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="High Burn with Short Runway">
  A portfolio company with less than 12 months of runway and no imminent financing event is in a precarious position. Review the most recent cash flow statement, confirm the current burn rate, and ask management for their fundraising plan and timeline. If the company is not actively fundraising, engage the team to discuss options, including bridge financing, burn reduction, or a strategic process.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Leadership Changes">
  A founder departure or the exit of a key executive (CEO, CFO, CTO) can significantly affect company trajectory. Schedule a call with the remaining management team as soon as the change is announced. Understand the circumstances of the departure, assess the transition plan, and determine whether the change affects Capria's board rights or governance position. Update the company profile to reflect the new leadership.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Missed Milestones">
  When a company misses a product launch date, revenue target, or strategic milestone that was documented in the investment memo or most recent update, request a written explanation and a revised plan. Distinguish between execution failures and external factors, and assess whether the miss changes the underlying investment thesis. Update the milestone log in the company profile.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Down Round">
  A new funding round priced below the valuation at which Capria invested indicates a loss in unrealized value. Immediately update the company's carrying value to reflect the new round price. Review the anti-dilution provisions in Capria's investment documents to understand any adjustment to the share count or conversion ratio. Note the down round in the investment record and flag it in the quarterly portfolio review.
</Accordion>

## Requesting Portfolio Data

Follow this process each quarter to collect the data needed for portfolio reviews, valuation updates, and LP reporting. Consistent execution of the data request process ensures that the knowledge base reflects current, verified information rather than stale estimates.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send the quarterly data request">
    Reach out to the portfolio company's CFO or CEO with a structured data request. Send the request at the start of each quarter, covering the prior quarter's performance. Use a standardized template that covers financials and operational KPIs so that companies know exactly what to provide.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect financial statements">
    Request the income statement (P&L), balance sheet, and cash flow statement for the most recently completed quarter. For early-stage companies that do not yet produce audited financials, management accounts are acceptable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect operational KPIs">
    Gather the operational metrics agreed upon at the time of investment and documented in the investment memo. Use the metric definitions from the tabs above to ensure consistency across reporting periods.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the company profile in the knowledge base">
    Enter the new financial and operational data into the portfolio company's profile. Update the valuation if a new mark is warranted. Log any significant events that occurred during the quarter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flag concerns for team review">
    If any of the red flags described above are present in the data received, note them explicitly in the company profile and add the company to the agenda for the next investment team review meeting. Do not wait until the formal quarterly review to raise urgent concerns.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Align on a standard data room template with each portfolio company at the time of investment — before the first quarterly data request arrives. Define the exact financial statements, KPI definitions, and reporting format you expect, and include this agreement in the post-investment onboarding checklist. Companies that know what to send from day one respond faster and provide cleaner data.
</Tip>
