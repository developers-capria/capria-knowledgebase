This page answers the most frequently asked questions from the Capria team about how to use the knowledge base and manage day-to-day fund and portfolio operations. If your question is not answered here, consult the relevant section of the knowledge base or reach out to the appropriate team contact listed on the [Support](/reference/support) page.

## Knowledge Base

<Accordion title="How often should I update portfolio company profiles?">
Update a portfolio company's profile after each material event, including:

- A new funding round (update valuation, ownership, cap table)
- A significant revenue or operational milestone
- A leadership change (CEO, CFO, or key co-founder)
- An exit or partial exit event

At a minimum, review and confirm all portfolio company profiles on a quarterly basis before LP reports are finalized and sent. Stale data in the knowledge base creates risk of LP reporting errors.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who has permission to update fund financial data?">
Fund-level financial data — including capital calls, distributions, management fees, and NAV — should only be updated by the **CFO** or a **designated finance team member** with explicit authorization. This data flows directly into LP capital account statements and performance metrics.

Portfolio company profiles, KPIs, and qualitative updates can be updated by the relevant **deal team member** responsible for that investment. If you are unsure whether a change falls under fund financials or portfolio company data, ask the CFO before making edits.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I handle a data discrepancy between the knowledge base and the fund administrator?">
Follow these steps when you identify a discrepancy:

1. Do not update the knowledge base until the discrepancy is resolved
2. Document the discrepancy: note the specific field, the knowledge base value, and the fund administrator's value
3. Flag the discrepancy to the **CFO** immediately
4. The CFO will work with the fund administrator to determine the authoritative figure
5. Once resolved, update the knowledge base to match the confirmed correct value

The **fund administrator's records are authoritative** for LP capital accounts and audited financial statements. Never send LP communications that contain figures that conflict with the fund administrator's records.
</Accordion>

## Fund Operations

<Accordion title="How much notice is required before a capital call?">
The LPA specifies the required notice period — **typically 10–15 business days** before the funding date. Always check the fund-specific LPA before issuing a capital call notice, as notice periods vary by fund and some LP side letters may include modified notice requirements.

Issue the capital call notice using the ILPA-format template, include LP-specific amounts and wire instructions, and send via the LP portal or encrypted email to the designated LP contact on record.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can we recall distributed capital?">
Only if the fund's LPA includes a **recycling provision**. Not all fund LPAs permit recycling. To check:

1. Review the fund's LPA for a recycling or reinvestment provision
2. Note the specific conditions: most recycling provisions only allow recalling capital from investments realized within the first few years of the fund (e.g., the first 3–5 years), and typically cap the total amount that can be recycled

If the LPA permits recycling and you intend to recall distributions, consult the General Counsel before issuing any notice to LPs, as the mechanics and limitations are fund-specific.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if an LP doesn't fund a capital call on time?">
Escalate immediately — do not wait. Take these steps as soon as a capital call funding deadline is missed:

1. Contact the LP directly (their designated wire/finance contact) to check for wire delays or technical issues
2. Notify the **CFO** on the same day the funding deadline is missed
3. Escalate to **legal counsel** if the LP has not funded within the cure period (typically 10 business days under the LPA)

The LPA will specify default provisions, which may include forfeiture of LP interest, forced sale of LP interest, or reduction of unfunded commitment. These are serious consequences — involve the CFO and legal counsel at the earliest sign of a funding issue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do we handle management fee offsets?">
Most LPAs include provisions requiring that fees paid to the GP or its affiliates by portfolio companies (monitoring fees, board fees, transaction fees, consulting fees) be offset against the management fee. The typical offset is 50–100% of such fees, as specified in the LPA.

To manage fee offsets correctly:

1. Track all portfolio company fees received in the fund's fee offset log
2. Apply the LPA-specified offset percentage to determine the reduction in management fee
3. Reflect the net management fee (after offsets) in LP fee disclosures and quarterly reports
4. Disclose all offsets in the ILPA Fee Transparency report included with the annual report

Failure to correctly apply and disclose fee offsets is a common source of LP disputes and regulatory scrutiny — maintain meticulous records.
</Accordion>

## Portfolio Management

<Accordion title="How do we value a portfolio company that hasn't raised a new round in 2+ years?">
Apply the **IPEV Guidelines** valuation methodology hierarchy. When there is no recent priced round, consider the following approaches in order of applicability:

1. **Comparable transaction multiples** — identify recent funding rounds for companies with similar business models, growth stage, and geography; apply those valuation multiples to the portfolio company's current metrics
2. **Revenue multiples** — if the company has meaningful, growing revenue, apply a market-derived revenue multiple (adjusted for growth rate, margin, and risk profile relative to comparables)
3. **Adjusted last-round valuation** — if neither of the above is practical, start from the last round valuation and adjust upward or downward based on material positive or negative developments since that round

Document the methodology, comparables used, and key assumptions in the valuation workpaper. The CFO must review and approve all valuations determined without a recent priced round before they are used in LP reporting.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What ownership percentage should we track — basic or fully diluted?">
Always track and report **fully diluted ownership** as the primary figure for all fund reporting purposes. Fully diluted ownership accounts for all issued shares plus the option pool, warrants, convertible notes, SAFEs, and any other instruments that could convert to equity.

Basic (non-diluted) ownership may be tracked separately as a reference figure but should never be used as the primary ownership percentage in LP reports, investment committee memos, or fund performance calculations. Using basic ownership overstates Capria's economic interest and creates inaccuracies in fund-level calculations.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do we record a write-off?">
Follow these steps to record a complete write-off of a portfolio company investment:

1. Update the portfolio company status in the knowledge base to **"Written Off"**
2. Set the carrying value to **$0**
3. Record the write-off date and the reason (company failure, insolvency, confirmed loss of value)
4. Obtain CFO approval for the write-off before updating fund NAV
5. Update the fund NAV to reflect the reduction (the write-off will reduce RVPI and TVPI accordingly)
6. Note the write-off in the next quarterly LP report with a brief explanation

Retain all investment documentation and write-off rationale in the fund's records — this documentation may be needed for audit, tax, or LP inquiry purposes.
</Accordion>

## LP Reporting

<Accordion title="What's the deadline for quarterly LP reports?">
Industry standard is **45–60 days after quarter-end**. Quarterly deadlines by standard timing are:

| Quarter End | Standard Report Deadline |
|---|---|
| March 31 | May 15 – May 30 |
| June 30 | August 14 – August 29 |
| September 30 | November 14 – November 29 |
| December 31 | February 14 – March 1 |

Always check **individual LP side letters** — some institutional LPs negotiate earlier reporting deadlines (e.g., 30 or 45 days after quarter-end) as a condition of their investment. Failing to meet side letter deadlines is a breach of agreement that must be avoided.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should we report gross or net performance to LPs?">
Always report **net IRR** and **net MOIC** as the primary performance figures in all LP communications. Net metrics reflect what LPs actually earn after management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest — they are the only figures that are directly relevant to LP investment returns.

Gross metrics may be included alongside net figures for context (e.g., to illustrate investment selection performance independent of fee structure), but they must be:

- Clearly and prominently labeled as "Gross"
- Presented alongside the corresponding net figure — never in isolation

Presenting gross performance without prominent net disclosure is misleading and inconsistent with ILPA and GIPS standards.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do we handle an LP requesting data beyond the standard quarterly report?">
Treat all ad hoc LP data requests carefully. Follow this process:

1. Acknowledge receipt of the request to the LP promptly (within 1–2 business days)
2. Escalate to the **GP and investor relations team** before gathering or preparing any data
3. Review the request for **confidentiality implications** — does it involve other LPs' data? Portfolio company confidential financials? Non-public information?
4. Review the LP's **side letter** for any enhanced reporting rights they may have negotiated
5. Respond with the approved data only, via the secure LP portal or encrypted email

Do not send ad hoc data informally (e.g., via unencrypted email or messaging) without investor relations review. If the request cannot be fulfilled as asked, explain why and offer an appropriate alternative.
</Accordion>
