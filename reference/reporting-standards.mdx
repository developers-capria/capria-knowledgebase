Capria's reporting standards are aligned with industry best practices, primarily the ILPA (Institutional Limited Partners Association) guidelines, to ensure transparency, comparability, and consistency in all LP communications. Understanding these standards helps every member of the team produce reports that meet institutional LP expectations and hold up to scrutiny from sophisticated investors.

## ILPA Reporting Standards

The Institutional Limited Partners Association has developed standardized templates and guidelines that form the backbone of Capria's LP reporting framework. ILPA standards address three primary areas:

- **ILPA Reporting Template (IRT)** — standardized quarterly report and capital call/distribution notice templates that improve comparability across funds and managers; Capria uses the IRT format for all standard quarterly and annual LP deliverables
- **ILPA Fee Transparency Initiative** — standardized fee and expense reporting that requires GPs to disclose all fees, expenses, and offsets at both the fund level and the portfolio company level; enables LPs to accurately assess net-of-fee returns
- **ILPA Diversity in Action** — a framework for voluntary disclosure of diversity, equity, and inclusion metrics at the GP level, including investment team composition and portfolio company diversity data

<Note>
  ILPA standards are voluntary — they are not legally required by regulation. However, adoption demonstrates a commitment to LP transparency and is expected by most institutional LPs (endowments, pensions, foundations). Deviating from ILPA templates without explanation may raise questions from LP due diligence teams.
</Note>

## GIPS (Global Investment Performance Standards)

GIPS is a set of voluntary global standards for investment performance reporting, developed and administered by the CFA Institute. Key characteristics of GIPS relevant to Capria's reporting:

- **Voluntary but highly credible** — GIPS compliance is not legally mandated but is a recognized mark of performance reporting integrity; it signals to LPs that performance figures have not been cherry-picked
- **Firm-wide compliance required** — a firm cannot claim GIPS compliance for select funds; compliance must apply to the entire firm's track record, which prevents selective presentation of only the best-performing funds
- **Fair representation and full disclosure** — GIPS requires presenting performance results in a way that is not misleading, including all funds in the relevant composite, and disclosing all material fee and calculation assumptions
- **Private equity provisions** — for VC funds, GIPS specifies that IRR and MOIC are the required return metrics; it defines how cash flows, NAV, and vintage year composites must be constructed and presented

## Standard Reporting Requirements

Capria produces the following standard reports in accordance with ILPA and fund LPA requirements. Confirm fund-specific deadlines in each fund's LPA and any LP side letters.

| Report | Standard | Frequency | Key Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quarterly Report** | ILPA IRT | Quarterly | Fund metrics (TVPI, DPI, RVPI, Net IRR), capital account statements, portfolio company updates, pacing analysis |
| **Annual Report** | ILPA IRT + Audited Financials | Annually | Audited financial statements, full fee and expense disclosure, complete portfolio review, GP commentary |
| **Capital Call Notice** | ILPA template | As needed | LP-specific call amounts, wire instructions, purpose of call, expected deployment timeline |
| **Distribution Notice** | ILPA template | As needed | LP-specific distribution amounts, source (company name and exit type), waterfall calculation detail |

## Valuation Standards

Capria applies the following valuation frameworks when determining the fair market value of portfolio company holdings for NAV calculations and LP reporting.

### ASC 820 (US GAAP Fair Value)

ASC 820 is the US GAAP accounting standard that defines fair value measurement for financial reporting purposes. Key points for VC application:

- **Definition of fair value** — the price that would be received to sell an asset in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date
- **Level 3 inputs** — private company valuations typically fall under Level 3 (unobservable inputs), requiring the use of management's best estimate based on available market data, comparable transactions, and internal models
- **Required for audited financials** — US GAAP-compliant audited financial statements require ASC 820 fair value measurement for all portfolio company holdings

### IPEV Guidelines (International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation Guidelines)

The IPEV Guidelines are a widely adopted international framework for determining the fair value of private equity and VC investments. Capria follows IPEV methodology for all quarterly carrying value determinations.

Apply IPEV valuation methodologies in the following order of preference:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Price of Recent Investment">
    Use the price from the most recent funding round as the basis for fair value. This is the most common methodology for early-stage companies. Assess whether any material changes since the last round warrant an upward or downward adjustment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Multiples of Revenue or EBITDA">
    Apply a market-derived revenue or EBITDA multiple to the company's current financials. Use this methodology for revenue-generating companies where comparable public company or transaction multiples are available. Document the selected multiple and its basis.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)">
    Apply a DCF model using projected free cash flows and an appropriate discount rate. Use this methodology for mature portfolio companies with predictable, recurring cash flows. Document key assumptions including growth rate, margin, and discount rate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Net Assets">
    Value the company based on the fair value of its net assets. Use this methodology for asset-heavy companies, holding companies, or pre-revenue companies where assets are the primary value driver.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Data Privacy and Confidentiality

All LP reporting data and portfolio company financial information is subject to strict confidentiality obligations. Apply the following standards consistently:

- **Portfolio company financial data is confidential** — do not share revenue, cost, or valuation information from one portfolio company with other portfolio companies, other LPs, or any external party without prior authorization
- **LP lists and commitment sizes are confidential** — do not disclose the identity of other LPs, their commitment amounts, or their capital account balances to other LPs or external parties
- **Report distribution must be controlled** — distribute quarterly and annual reports only to the intended LP recipient via the LP portal or encrypted email; do not forward or share reports with third parties
- **Individual login credentials are required** — LP portal access must use individual, named login credentials; shared logins are prohibited and prevent proper audit trail maintenance

<Warning>
  Sharing confidential fund or portfolio data externally without authorization may violate the LPA, applicable securities laws (including SEC regulations on material non-public information), and your contractual obligations to LPs. If you are unsure whether a data request is appropriate to fulfill, escalate to the General Counsel before responding.
</Warning>
