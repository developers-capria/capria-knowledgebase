This glossary provides clear definitions for the key terms used throughout the Capria knowledge base — from fund mechanics to portfolio management vocabulary — ensuring everyone on the team and among our LPs speaks the same language. Use this as your reference whenever you encounter an unfamiliar term in a report, LPA, or internal document.

## A

<Accordion title="Anti-dilution Protection">
A contractual right that protects an investor from dilution in a down round. The two most common forms are:

- **Broad-based weighted average** — adjusts the conversion price of preferred stock based on a weighted average of all outstanding shares; the most LP-friendly and commonly used form
- **Full ratchet** — adjusts the conversion price to the new, lower round price regardless of the size of the down round; more aggressive and investor-favorable
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)">
Annualized revenue from recurring subscriptions or contracts. Calculated by multiplying MRR × 12, or by summing all active annual contract values. ARR is the primary top-line metric for SaaS and subscription businesses and is frequently used in portfolio company KPI reporting.
</Accordion>

## B

<Accordion title="Bridge Financing">
Short-term funding extended to a company to extend its runway while a larger, more permanent round is being prepared. Bridge financings are often structured as convertible notes or SAFEs and convert into the subsequent priced round. They carry execution risk if the anticipated round does not materialize.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Burn Rate">
The rate at which a company spends its cash reserves, typically expressed on a monthly basis. Two common variants:

- **Gross burn** — total monthly cash expenditures
- **Net burn** — monthly cash out minus monthly cash in (revenue); the more meaningful measure of runway consumption

Runway (months) = Cash Balance / Net Monthly Burn Rate
</Accordion>

## C

<Accordion title="Called Capital">
The portion of committed capital that has been formally requested (drawn down) from LPs via capital calls. Called capital equals paid-in capital once LPs have funded the call. Not all committed capital is called at fund inception — it is drawn as investments are made.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Cap Table (Capitalization Table)">
A spreadsheet or ledger showing a company's full equity ownership structure. A cap table lists all shareholders (founders, employees, investors), the number and class of shares each holds, and the resulting ownership percentages on both a basic and fully diluted basis. Maintaining an accurate, up-to-date cap table is essential for tracking ownership and modeling dilution.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Carried Interest (Carry)">
The GP's share of investment profits, typically 20% of returns above the hurdle rate. Carry is the primary performance incentive for fund managers. It is paid only after LPs have received back their paid-in capital plus the preferred return (hurdle rate). The specific carry percentage and calculation methodology are defined in the LPA.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Co-investment">
A direct investment made by an LP alongside the fund in a specific portfolio company. Co-investments are typically offered to select LPs on a deal-by-deal basis, often with reduced or no management fees and carry. They allow LPs to increase their exposure to high-conviction investments beyond their pro-rata fund allocation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Committed Capital">
The total capital pledged by all LPs to the fund, as documented in their subscription agreements. Committed capital is not necessarily deployed immediately — it is drawn down via capital calls over the investment period. Total committed capital equals the fund size.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Convertible Note">
A debt instrument that converts to equity at a future priced round. Convertible notes accrue interest and include key conversion terms such as a valuation cap (maximum conversion valuation) and a discount rate (percentage below the new round price at which the note converts). They are commonly used for seed and bridge financings where setting a valuation is impractical.
</Accordion>

## D

<Accordion title="DPI (Distributions to Paid-In)">
A realized return multiple that measures cumulative distributions to LPs as a multiple of total paid-in capital. DPI is the primary measure of actual, realized fund performance — sometimes called "cash-on-cash return." A DPI above 1.0x means LPs have received back more than their invested capital from realized exits.

```
DPI = Cumulative Distributions to LPs / Total Paid-In Capital
```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Down Round">
A funding round priced at a lower valuation than the company's previous financing round. Down rounds are dilutive to existing shareholders and can trigger anti-dilution protection provisions for earlier investors. They often signal deteriorating company performance or adverse market conditions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Drawdown">
See also: Capital Call. The act of requesting LP capital contributions pursuant to the fund's investment or operational needs. LPs are required to fund drawdowns within the notice period specified in the LPA (typically 10–15 business days).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Dry Powder">
Uncalled committed capital that remains available for future investments. Dry powder equals committed capital minus called capital. Monitoring dry powder levels is critical for pacing analysis and ensuring the fund has sufficient capital to execute its investment strategy and fund follow-on reserves.
</Accordion>

## E

<Accordion title="EBITDA">
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. A widely used proxy for operational cash flow and profitability. EBITDA removes the effects of capital structure (interest), tax jurisdiction, and non-cash charges (depreciation and amortization) to allow for comparisons across companies and industries. Commonly used in revenue-multiple and EBITDA-multiple valuation analyses for growth-stage and mature portfolio companies.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Exit">
A liquidity event through which the fund realizes value from a portfolio company investment. Common exit routes include:

- **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** — company lists on a public stock exchange
- **M&A (Merger & Acquisition)** — company is acquired by a strategic or financial buyer
- **Secondary sale** — the fund sells its stake to another investor prior to a broader liquidity event
</Accordion>

## F

<Accordion title="Follow-on Investment">
A subsequent investment by the fund into an existing portfolio company, made after the initial entry investment. Follow-on investments are typically made to maintain pro-rata ownership in up rounds, support a bridge, or double down on high-performing companies. Follow-on reserves are budgeted as part of the fund's reserve ratio.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Fund of Funds">
A pooled investment vehicle that allocates capital to a portfolio of underlying funds rather than investing directly in companies. Fund of funds (FoF) structures add a layer of diversification and professional fund selection but also introduce an additional layer of fees (management fee and carry at both the FoF and underlying fund level).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Fund Life">
The total contractual lifespan of a fund, typically 10 years. Fund life is generally structured as:

- **Investment period** — typically years 1–5, during which new investments are made
- **Harvest period** — typically years 6–10, during which existing investments are managed toward exit

Most LPAs allow for one or two one-year extensions with LP consent.
</Accordion>

## G

<Accordion title="GP (General Partner)">
The fund manager — the entity or individuals who make investment decisions, manage the fund, and are legally responsible for fund operations. The GP has unlimited liability and earns management fees and carried interest. In the context of Capria, the GP refers to Capria's management company and its investment professionals.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Gross IRR">
The internal rate of return calculated before deducting management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest. Gross IRR reflects the investment performance of the portfolio itself, independent of the fund's fee structure. It is useful for evaluating investment selection skill but is not the return LPs actually receive.
</Accordion>

## H

<Accordion title="Hurdle Rate">
The minimum preferred return that LPs must receive before the GP earns carried interest. The hurdle rate is typically set at 8% IRR per annum (compounded). Once LPs have received their paid-in capital plus the hurdle return, the GP begins to participate in profits via carry, subject to any catch-up provision in the LPA.
</Accordion>

## I

<Accordion title="IRR (Internal Rate of Return)">
The discount rate at which the net present value of all cash flows (capital calls and distributions) equals zero. IRR is a time-weighted return metric — earlier distributions have a disproportionately positive effect, and later capital calls have a disproportionately negative effect. It is the standard return metric for VC fund performance.

```
NPV = Σ [Cash Flow_t / (1 + IRR)^t] = 0
```
</Accordion>

## J

<Accordion title="J-Curve">
The characteristic pattern of VC fund returns over time — early negative returns followed by increasingly positive returns as the fund matures and investments are realized. In the early years, management fees are drawn and initial investments are marked at cost or below, causing NAV to dip. As companies grow and exits occur, the fund's value rises above the initial capital invested, creating the characteristic "J" shape on a return vs. time chart.
</Accordion>

## L

<Accordion title="LP (Limited Partner)">
An investor in the fund who has limited liability (capped at their committed capital) and plays a passive investment role. LPs do not participate in day-to-day investment decisions. Common LP types include endowments, pension funds, family offices, foundations, sovereign wealth funds, and high-net-worth individuals.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="LPA (Limited Partnership Agreement)">
The governing legal document of the fund. The LPA defines the rights and obligations of the GP and LPs, including fund economics (fees, carry, hurdle), investment restrictions, reporting requirements, LP consent rights, and fund governance. The LPA is the authoritative reference for all fund operations questions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Lead Investor">
The investor who anchors a funding round, sets the key terms of the term sheet, and typically takes a board seat or board observer right. The lead investor performs primary due diligence and is responsible for negotiating deal terms on behalf of the investor syndicate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Liquidation Preference">
The contractual right of preferred stockholders to receive their invested capital (and often a multiple thereof) before common stockholders receive any proceeds in a liquidation event (sale, dissolution, or wind-down). A 1x non-participating liquidation preference is standard; participating preferred shares receive their preference plus a pro-rata share of the remaining proceeds.
</Accordion>

## M

<Accordion title="Management Fee">
An annual fee paid by LPs to the GP to cover the fund's operating expenses, including staff salaries, office, travel, legal, and other overhead. Management fees are typically 2% of committed capital during the investment period, stepping down to 1.5% or 2% of net invested capital during the harvest period. The exact fee structure is specified in the LPA.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital)">
A time-agnostic return multiple that measures total value (realized plus unrealized) relative to total invested capital. Unlike IRR, MOIC does not account for the time value of money — a 3.0x MOIC over 3 years is significantly better than a 3.0x MOIC over 10 years.

```
MOIC = (Realized Value + Unrealized Value) / Total Invested Capital
```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)">
Monthly revenue from recurring subscriptions or contracts. MRR is the foundational SaaS metric, typically broken into New MRR (new customers), Expansion MRR (upsells), Churned MRR (cancellations), and Net New MRR. ARR = MRR × 12.
</Accordion>

## N

<Accordion title="NAV (Net Asset Value)">
The current fair market value of all fund assets (portfolio company holdings at carrying value, plus cash and receivables) minus fund liabilities. NAV is the primary measure of a fund's unrealized value at any point in time. It is reported quarterly in LP statements and used in RVPI and TVPI calculations.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Net IRR">
The internal rate of return after deducting management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest. Net IRR is the actual return LPs receive on their invested capital. Always use net IRR for LP communications; gross IRR may be shown alongside for context but must be clearly labeled.
</Accordion>

## P

<Accordion title="Paid-In Capital">
The total capital that has been formally called and actually received from LPs. Paid-in capital equals called capital once LPs have funded their contributions. It is the denominator in all DPI, RVPI, and TVPI calculations.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="PME (Public Market Equivalent)">
A benchmark methodology that compares fund performance to a public market index (e.g., MSCI Emerging Markets, S&P 500) by discounting fund cash flows using index returns. The most common variant is the KS-PME (Kaplan-Schoar). A PME above 1.0x indicates the fund outperformed the benchmark index on a risk-adjusted basis.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Portfolio Company">
A company in which the fund has made an equity (or equity-linked) investment. Portfolio companies are tracked individually in the knowledge base with profiles covering investment details, financials, KPIs, and valuation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Post-money Valuation">
The company's valuation immediately after a funding round closes — calculated as pre-money valuation plus the new capital invested. Post-money valuation determines the ownership percentage received by new investors in the round.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pre-money Valuation">
The company's valuation immediately before a funding round closes. Pre-money valuation is a key negotiated term and is used to calculate the price per share and resulting ownership for new investors.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pro-rata Rights">
The contractual right for an existing investor to participate in future funding rounds in proportion to their current ownership stake. Exercising pro-rata rights prevents dilution. Pro-rata rights may be contractual (set in the investment agreement) or major investor pro-rata rights (available only above a threshold ownership percentage).
</Accordion>

## R

<Accordion title="Reserve Ratio">
The portion of total fund capital set aside for follow-on investments in existing portfolio companies, rather than new investments. A typical reserve ratio is 40–50% of total fund capital. Maintaining appropriate reserves ensures the fund can protect ownership in its best-performing companies in subsequent rounds.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="RVPI (Residual Value to Paid-In)">
A return multiple measuring the unrealized (remaining) portfolio value relative to paid-in capital. RVPI represents the "paper" or unrealized portion of fund returns and equals TVPI minus DPI.

```
RVPI = Current NAV / Total Paid-In Capital
```
</Accordion>

## S

<Accordion title="SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)">
A convertible instrument pioneered by Y Combinator that provides an investor with the right to receive equity at a future priced round. Unlike a convertible note, a SAFE is not debt — it does not accrue interest or have a maturity date. SAFEs include a valuation cap and/or discount rate and convert automatically at the next qualifying priced round.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Secondary Sale">
A transaction in which an LP sells their fund interest to a third-party buyer (LP secondary), or in which a fund sells its stake in a portfolio company to another investor prior to a full exit (direct secondary). Secondary transactions provide liquidity before the fund's natural exit timeline but typically occur at a discount to NAV.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Side Letter">
A separate legal agreement between the GP and a specific LP that grants that LP negotiated terms beyond the standard LPA. Common side letter provisions include most-favored-nation (MFN) clauses, co-investment rights, reduced management fees or carry, enhanced reporting, and ERISA considerations. Side letters must be consistent with the LPA.
</Accordion>

## T

<Accordion title="Term Sheet">
A non-binding document that outlines the key economic and governance terms and conditions of a proposed investment. Term sheets cover valuation, investment amount, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, board composition, and protective provisions. Once signed, the parties proceed to definitive documentation (stock purchase agreement, investor rights agreement, etc.).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="TVPI (Total Value to Paid-In)">
The primary fund performance multiple, measuring total value (realized distributions plus unrealized NAV) relative to total paid-in capital. TVPI = DPI + RVPI. Early in a fund's life, TVPI is driven by RVPI (unrealized); in a mature fund, DPI dominates.

```
TVPI = (Cumulative Distributions + Current NAV) / Total Paid-In Capital
```
</Accordion>

## V

<Accordion title="Valuation Cap">
The maximum company valuation at which a SAFE or convertible note converts to equity. The valuation cap protects early investors by ensuring they receive a minimum ownership percentage regardless of how high the company's valuation is at the priced round. A lower cap is more favorable for the investor.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Vintage Year">
The year a fund made its first investment (or sometimes the year of final close). Vintage year is the standard basis for peer benchmarking — funds are compared against other funds from the same vintage, since market conditions at the time of deployment significantly affect returns.
</Accordion>

## W

<Accordion title="Waterfall">
The contractual order in which fund distributions are allocated between LPs and the GP. A typical VC waterfall: (1) return of paid-in capital to LPs, (2) preferred return to LPs at the hurdle rate, (3) GP catch-up (GP receives a disproportionate share until it has received its full carry percentage of total profits), (4) carried interest split (typically 80% LP / 20% GP) on remaining profits. The specific waterfall is defined in the LPA.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Write-down">
A reduction in the carrying value of a portfolio company investment below its previous carrying value, reflecting a decline in the company's fair market value. A write-down reduces fund NAV and RVPI but is not a realized loss until an exit occurs. Write-downs should be documented with supporting valuation rationale per IPEV guidelines.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Write-off">
A complete reduction of a portfolio company's carrying value to zero, reflecting a total loss of the investment. A write-off occurs when the company has failed, is insolvent, or the fund's equity is determined to have no recoverable value. Write-offs reduce fund NAV, RVPI, and TVPI and are recorded as realized losses.
</Accordion>
