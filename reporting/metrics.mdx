Performance metrics are the primary language of VC fund reporting. Whether you are presenting results to LPs, evaluating a new investment opportunity, or benchmarking Capria's funds against peers, fluency in these measures is essential. This page defines each metric, explains how it is calculated, and clarifies when to use it — so the team communicates consistently with LPs and evaluates funds on the same basis across every reporting cycle.

## Return Multiples

Return multiples express fund performance as a ratio of value returned relative to capital invested. They are simple, intuitive, and time-agnostic — making them easy to compare across investments regardless of cash flow timing.

### MOIC — Multiple on Invested Capital

MOIC measures the total value generated by a fund or investment relative to the capital deployed into it. It combines both realized proceeds (from exits) and unrealized value (current NAV of remaining holdings) and divides that sum by the total capital invested.

```text
MOIC = (Realized Value + Unrealized Value) / Invested Capital
```

**Example:** If Capria invests $10M into a portfolio company, receives $8M in realized distributions from a partial exit, and the remaining stake carries a current fair value of $12M, the MOIC is ($8M + $12M) / $10M = **2.0x**.

<Note>
  MOIC does not account for the time value of money. A 2.0x return achieved in two years is far more valuable than the same 2.0x achieved in ten years. Always read MOIC alongside IRR for a complete picture of performance.
</Note>

### TVPI — Total Value to Paid-In

TVPI is the most comprehensive value multiple. It captures the full picture of a fund's performance by combining both what has already been distributed to LPs (DPI) and the estimated current value of what remains in the portfolio (RVPI).

```text
TVPI = (Cumulative Distributions + Residual NAV) / Paid-In Capital
```

Because TVPI incorporates unrealized value, it is sensitive to portfolio company valuations. As the fund matures and unrealized positions are exited, TVPI converges toward DPI.

### DPI — Distributions to Paid-In

DPI measures only the realized returns that have been distributed back to LPs. It is the clearest signal of cash-on-cash performance and the metric LPs watch most closely in the later years of a fund.

```text
DPI = Cumulative Distributions to LPs / Paid-In Capital
```

<Note>
  Early in a fund's life, DPI is typically low or near zero — capital is being deployed rather than returned. A DPI greater than 1.0x is a significant milestone: it means LPs have received back more cash than they originally invested. DPI is the most credible measure of fund performance because it reflects actual cash returned.
</Note>

### RVPI — Residual Value to Paid-In

RVPI represents the unrealized portion of a fund's value — the current estimated worth of portfolio companies that have not yet been fully exited, expressed as a multiple of paid-in capital.

```text
RVPI = Residual Portfolio NAV / Paid-In Capital
```

<Note>
  RVPI decreases over the fund life as companies are exited and proceeds are distributed to LPs. In early-stage funds, RVPI can remain elevated for many years until the exit cycle begins. High RVPI late in a fund's life may indicate difficulty in achieving liquidity events.
</Note>

## Time-Weighted Return

### IRR — Internal Rate of Return

IRR is the annualized discount rate that makes the net present value (NPV) of all fund cash flows — capital calls (negative) and distributions (positive) — equal to zero. Unlike MOIC, IRR accounts for the timing of cash flows: early distributions and late capital calls improve IRR, while delayed distributions and early calls reduce it.

<Warning>
  IRR can be misleading early in a fund's life due to the **J-curve effect**. In the first few years, fees and write-downs depress returns before portfolio companies have had time to mature and generate exits. An early IRR that looks negative or very low does not indicate a failing fund. Always read IRR alongside MOIC to get a full picture, and exercise caution when comparing IRRs across funds at different stages of maturity.
</Warning>

Capria reports both gross and net IRR in all fund performance disclosures:

- **Gross IRR** — Calculated before management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest. Reflects the fund's investment performance at the portfolio level and is most useful for evaluating the investment team's skill in selecting and managing companies.
- **Net IRR** — Calculated after all management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest. This is what LPs actually receive and is the standard basis for LP reporting, benchmarking, and fundraising comparisons.

Always specify gross or net basis whenever you report IRR. Presenting gross IRR to LPs without clear disclosure is misleading and inconsistent with ILPA reporting standards.

### PME — Public Market Equivalent

PME benchmarks a private fund's performance against what an investor would have earned by investing the same capital in a public market index over the same period. This is particularly valuable for emerging market funds where VC-specific peer benchmarks may be limited or unreliable.

Capria uses the **Kaplan-Schoar PME (KS-PME)** method:

```text
KS-PME = PV of Distributions / PV of Capital Calls
         (using index returns as the discount rate)
```

Each capital call and distribution is discounted back to the present using the cumulative return of the chosen index (e.g., MSCI Emerging Markets) over the same time period. A **KS-PME greater than 1.0x** means the fund outperformed the index on a risk-adjusted, time-weighted basis. A PME below 1.0x means the LP would have done better investing in the index.

## Metric Summary Table

Use the table below as a quick reference when selecting the right metric for a given reporting or analysis context.

| Metric | Measures | Accounts for Time | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOIC | Total value multiple | No | Quick comparison across investments |
| TVPI | Total value (realized + unrealized) | No | Overall fund value assessment |
| DPI | Realized returns only | No | Assessing how much has been returned to LPs |
| RVPI | Unrealized value | No | Understanding remaining portfolio upside |
| IRR (Gross) | Time-weighted return before fees | Yes | Evaluating fund manager investment skill |
| IRR (Net) | Time-weighted return after fees | Yes | LP perspective on actual returns received |
| PME | Performance vs. public market index | Yes | Benchmarking against public market alternatives |

## Reporting Periods

All performance metrics must be calculated as of a specific reporting date and clearly labeled in every deliverable — whether an LP report, an internal review, or a board presentation. Unlabeled metrics create confusion and erode LP trust.

Every metric disclosure should include:

- **As-of date** — The quarter-end or year-end date the figures reflect (e.g., "As of September 30, 2024")
- **Gross vs. net basis** — Explicitly stated for all IRR figures; net is the default for LP-facing materials
- **Vintage year** — The year in which the fund began deploying capital, which is required context for any benchmarking comparison

Metrics are typically refreshed quarterly, aligned to standard quarter-end dates (March 31, June 30, September 30, December 31). Annual reports include an additional layer of audited financial validation.
