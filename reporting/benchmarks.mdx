Benchmarking allows the team and LPs to assess Capria's performance in context — not just in absolute terms, but relative to peer funds of the same vintage year, strategy, and geography. A 2.0x MOIC or a 15% net IRR means very little without knowing whether comparable funds achieved the same or better results over the same period and market conditions. Understanding how to select the right benchmark, interpret the results, and present them honestly to LPs is a core skill for anyone involved in fund reporting or LP relations at Capria.

## Why Benchmarking Matters

LPs do not evaluate fund managers in isolation. They evaluate you relative to every other fund manager competing for the same capital allocation. A significant portion of that evaluation is driven by **quartile ranking** — where your fund's net IRR and MOIC stand relative to a universe of comparable funds. Institutional LPs, in particular, have investment committee mandates that often require a fund to demonstrate top-quartile or upper-median performance to receive a re-up commitment.

Top-quartile performance (Q1) is the standard that Capria aims for and that LPs use as a threshold for continued relationships and participation in future fundraises. Falling below the median for an extended period without a compelling explanation will raise questions — not just about returns, but about strategy, portfolio construction, and team quality.

## Quartile Rankings

The VC industry benchmarks fund performance by dividing a peer universe of comparable funds into four quartiles based on net IRR (and sometimes net MOIC). Understand where each quartile sits and what it signals:

- **Q1 (Top Quartile)** — The top 25% of peer funds by performance. This is Capria's target for every fund. Top-quartile funds command the strongest LP loyalty, the most favorable fund terms in subsequent raises, and the clearest differentiation in a competitive fundraising environment.
- **Q2 (Second Quartile)** — The 25th to 50th percentile. Acceptable performance, but not differentiating. LPs may continue their relationship, but are unlikely to increase allocation, and the fund will face more scrutiny during re-up conversations.
- **Q3 (Third Quartile)** — The 50th to 75th percentile, meaning below median. Performance at this level warrants a clear explanation to LPs — ideally tied to specific, identifiable factors (market dislocation, portfolio concentration risk) rather than general market conditions.
- **Q4 (Bottom Quartile)** — The bottom 25% of peer funds. This is a serious concern for LP relationships, future fundraising, and in some cases GP/LP governance. A fund in the bottom quartile after sufficient maturity requires an honest conversation with LPs and a rigorous internal review.

<Note>
  Quartile rankings are most meaningful after **year 5** of a fund's life, when the J-curve has largely resolved and the portfolio is mature enough to reflect actual performance rather than early-stage markdowns and fee drag. Do not over-interpret early-year quartile rankings — a fund that appears in Q3 at year 2 may be Q1 by year 7 as exits accumulate.
</Note>

## Benchmark Databases

Capria uses the following industry-standard databases to obtain benchmark data. Each covers a different scope and provides different metrics — use the most appropriate source for the specific fund and peer group being benchmarked.

| Database | Coverage | Key Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge Associates | Global VC and PE | IRR, MOIC, quartile benchmarks by vintage year, geography, and stage |
| Preqin | Global VC and PE | IRR, MOIC, PME, fund-level data with LP relationship detail |
| PitchBook | Global VC | Round-level data, valuations, comparable transactions, and market trends |
| ILPA (Institutional Limited Partners Association) | LP-side standards | Reporting templates, fee transparency standards, and governance guidance |
| Burgiss | Global PE/VC | Cash flow-based benchmarks, PME analysis, portfolio-level analytics |

## Benchmark Peer Group Selection

The quality of a benchmark depends entirely on the quality of the peer group. Selecting the wrong peer group produces a misleading picture — either flattering or unfairly harsh — and will be challenged by sophisticated LPs who understand the methodology. When constructing a peer group for Capria fund benchmarking, apply the following criteria:

- **Same vintage year** — Match funds with a vintage year within ± 1–2 years of the fund being benchmarked. Vintage year is typically defined as the year of first capital call or first close.
- **Same strategy** — Compare like-for-like: early-stage vs. early-stage, growth equity vs. growth equity, or sector-specific vs. sector-specific. Mixing early-stage and growth funds in the same peer group distorts IRR and MOIC comparisons significantly.
- **Same geography** — Geography is especially important for Capria given its focus on emerging markets. Use peer groups that match the fund's primary geographic mandate (e.g., Sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, South Asia, pan-Emerging Markets) rather than defaulting to global VC benchmarks.
- **Similar fund size** — Compare funds within roughly 1–2x of Capria's fund size. A $50M fund and a $500M fund operate in fundamentally different parts of the market, and their IRR profiles are not directly comparable.

<Warning>
  Comparing an emerging markets fund against US VC benchmarks will almost always produce misleading results. US VC data is dominated by Silicon Valley technology funds with access to deep domestic exit markets, high valuations, and a mature LP ecosystem. Using these benchmarks for an Africa- or Southeast Asia-focused fund sets an inappropriate standard and obscures rather than illuminates actual performance. Always match the peer group to the fund's actual strategy and geography.
</Warning>

## PME Benchmarking

Public Market Equivalent (PME) analysis provides an alternative benchmarking approach that does not rely on peer fund databases. Instead of comparing against other private funds, PME measures whether LPs would have done better simply investing in a public market index over the same period with the same cash flows.

PME is particularly valuable for Capria's emerging market funds, where VC-specific peer data in Cambridge Associates or Preqin may be limited, lagged, or drawn from too small a universe to be statistically meaningful. PME gives LPs and the team a clear, intuitive answer to the question: "Did the illiquidity premium justify investing in this fund?"

Use the following indices as PME benchmarks depending on the fund's geography and mandate:

- **MSCI Emerging Markets Index** — The primary benchmark for Capria's emerging markets-focused funds; covers large- and mid-cap equities across 24 emerging market countries
- **MSCI Frontier Markets Index** — Appropriate for funds with significant allocation to frontier economies where the MSCI EM index has limited coverage
- **S&P 500** — Use for US-comparable performance context or when reporting to US-based LPs who use this as their opportunity cost baseline
- **Russell 2000** — Useful for small-cap comparison, particularly when the portfolio skews toward smaller companies and earlier stages

## Presenting Benchmark Data to LPs

Benchmark data is only useful if it is presented clearly, honestly, and with full methodological transparency. Sophisticated LPs will scrutinize benchmark choices and call out any presentation that appears to cherry-pick favorable comparisons. Follow these best practices in all LP-facing benchmark disclosures:

- **Always disclose the data source** and the specific peer group used (database, vintage year range, geography filter, stage filter, and number of funds in the universe)
- **Show the quartile ranking and the size of the peer universe** — a Q1 ranking among 12 funds carries far less weight than Q1 among 150 funds
- **Report on a net IRR and net MOIC basis** — never present gross metrics in benchmark comparisons without explicit disclosure, as the comparison is not valid on a like-for-like basis
- **Label the as-of date of the benchmark data** — benchmark databases update over time as more funds report; specify the data vintage (e.g., "Cambridge Associates data as of Q4 2023")
- **Acknowledge that vintage-year benchmarks evolve** — early data for a given vintage year is based on fewer funds and will shift as more funds mature and report; note this limitation when presenting benchmarks for recent vintages

<Tip>
  Update benchmark data on an annual basis, timed to coincide with annual report preparation. This ensures that the benchmark data LPs receive in the annual report reflects the most current available peer universe and that any shifts in Capria's quartile ranking are identified and addressed proactively before the annual LP meeting.
</Tip>
